# Home ocean artwork

The home surface uses a painted ocean plate instead of the previous flat SVG wave bands and outlined light grid. It retains the existing sky, shore, Nimo entrance, camera and boat-contact effects. `home-ocean-water.js` renders the plate through a small native WebGL shader: crossing swell fields deform different parts of the water independently, with finer ripples and changing slope highlights. It does not translate the entire image as a single layer.

The shared `NextSkillMotion` policy stops the GPU loop when paused, hidden, outside the scene or covered by a dialog. Resume continues the same phase. Reduced motion, unavailable WebGL or a lost context retain the complete static artwork. The pixel ratio is capped at 1.5 and surface width at 2048 pixels; drawing is capped at 60 frames per second. Night lighting and celestial transitions still use the existing theme controller. `scripts/verify-home-water.cjs` checks changing GPU pixels, fixed image placement, pause/resume, dialogs, tab lifecycle, both themes, reduced motion and unsupported-GPU fallback across six viewport sizes.

The main boat now disturbs that same water material. Hull measurements use the existing 480 × 280 artwork's immersed keel; camera-relative velocity sets the wake direction and strength. The shader refracts water around the bow and a widening trailing wake. A second lightweight canvas draws broken foam and bubbles from a fixed pool of 48 packets. Each packet retains its position in scene coordinates and fades over 3.2 seconds, leaving a trail when the camera moves. Idle rocking remains below the emission threshold. Resize/teleport clears stale geometry; reduced motion clears the trail, and ordinary pause freezes its phase. No new animation clock is added. `scripts/verify-boat-wake.cjs` checks real entrance, foam pixels, pause, settling, camera-relative sailing, reversal, bounded packets and reduced motion at six viewport sizes.
