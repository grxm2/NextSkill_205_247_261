# Home ocean artwork

The home surface uses a painted ocean plate instead of the previous flat SVG wave bands and outlined light grid. It retains the existing sky, shore, Nimo entrance, camera and boat-contact effects. `home-ocean-water.js` renders the plate through a small native WebGL shader: crossing swell fields deform different parts of the water independently, with finer ripples and changing slope highlights. It does not translate the entire image as a single layer.

Pale painted foam receives a selective tint toward the surrounding turquoise, capped at 20%, and slope/gleam intensity is reduced. This softens the brightest fine edges without blurring the wave volumes, changing the artwork, or altering motion and world registration.

Home refraction amplitude is reduced by roughly one fifth, with the same wave phases and speed. A 4% painted-contrast roll-off softens sculpted ridges, and a broader, weaker glint plus slightly lighter reflection overlays reduces the gel-like material impression. These adjustments are scoped to Home; camera registration, coastal anchors and other pages are unaffected.

The material uses the exact inverse translation and scale of `.ow-camera`, shared by the island and temple. The sea controller records the original waterline in `data-world-top`; the visible sea can rise at the harbor without changing that material origin or its scale. The mobile image crop also uses these stable world dimensions. There is no depth-dependent camera shift or continuous translation of the entire painted image: both made the water slide beneath otherwise stationary land. Crossing local swells, fine ripples and changing slope light keep the surface visibly animated while the structural artwork stays in world coordinates. Manual mirrored texture addressing extends the original non-power-of-two painting without clamped edge streaks on long or reverse voyages. The old mist and looping foam sheets are hidden on Home so they do not compete with this surface.

When a rising harbor horizon exposes water above the original painting, a soft low-contrast distant-water continuation fills that area in the same world coordinates. It avoids vertically stretching or mirroring the image's top pixel row into a band.

Three soft coastal footprints follow the island's actual SVG bounds and the temple's two shores. They attenuate displacement and lighting at the contact edges, blending into fully animated open water outside. Existing shoreline wash effects stay attached to the artwork, and the temple's central canal remains open. These footprints do not move the land or replace the source images.

The sky-to-water mask is 14px instead of 48px, with a low-contrast distant-water tint and a faded coastline base. This keeps a readable horizon without a broad fog gap. The blend also covers static artwork and has a night variant; the source painting is unchanged.

The distant boats, buoy and whale share the same camera transform as the sea and land, with no extra backdrop parallax or voyage speed multiplier. The boats use the rendered sea's actual top edge, converted to backdrop coordinates by `alignBackdrop()` in `ocean-world.js`, rather than a separately tweened percentage. Their contact line sits 30px below that edge, past the transparent horizon strip. Hull bobbing is limited to .35px with .65-degree roll, the lowest hull edge is clipped into its stronger meniscus, and the shadow stays on the water plane. The same alignment follows sea height changes at the harbor and responsive or static layout changes.

The shared `NextSkillMotion` policy stops the GPU loop when paused, hidden, outside the scene or covered by a dialog. Resume continues the same phase. Reduced motion, unavailable WebGL or a lost context retain the complete static artwork. The pixel ratio is capped at 1.5 and surface width at 2048 pixels; drawing is capped at 60 frames per second. Night lighting and celestial transitions still use the existing theme controller. `scripts/verify-home-water.cjs` checks changing GPU pixels, fixed image placement, pause/resume, dialogs, tab lifecycle, both themes, reduced motion and unsupported-GPU fallback across six viewport sizes.

The main boat now disturbs that same water material. Hull measurements use the existing 480 × 280 artwork's immersed keel; camera-relative velocity sets the wake direction and strength. The shader refracts water around the bow and a widening trailing wake. A second lightweight canvas draws broken foam and bubbles from a fixed pool of 48 packets. Each packet retains its position in world coordinates and fades over 3.2 seconds, leaving a trail when the camera moves. Packets and velocity measurement share the water's inverse camera transform, including scale. Idle rocking remains below the emission threshold. Resize/teleport clears stale geometry; reduced motion clears the trail, and ordinary pause freezes its phase. `nextskill:ocean-view` reprojects the frozen surface when the user scrolls while ambient motion is paused; it does not advance time or emit foam. No new animation clock is added. `scripts/verify-boat-wake.cjs` checks real entrance, foam pixels, pause, settling, camera-relative sailing, reversal, bounded packets and reduced motion at six viewport sizes.

`scripts/verify-water-world.cjs` pauses ambient time and samples GPU water patches using the island's actual DOM movement and the temple SVG's actual coordinates. It checks matching motion at every water depth, vertical registration while the horizon rises, reverse travel and covered edges at the far harbor. The expected positions come from the artwork, not a duplicate of the shader's projection formula. Development-only pixel sampling uses the existing scenery QA hook. Reports and screenshots are written under ignored `artifacts/world-water-qa/`.

`scripts/verify-water-contact.cjs` checks visible changes within one second while the camera rests and all seven distant keels against their contact lines at shore, sea and harbor, across six viewport sizes. It defaults to the local IIS HTTPS origin `https://localhost:44389`; `NEXTSKILL_TEST_URL` can override it. Preview video and reports are under ignored `artifacts/wave-contact-qa/`.
